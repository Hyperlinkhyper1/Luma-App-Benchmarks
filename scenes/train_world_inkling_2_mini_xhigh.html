How the signalling works ▸

The track is split into blocks — a block holds at most one train (any car). A route is the reservation of the blocks ahead: when a train enters a block it reserves the next three, so the (single-track) main line and the passing loop are never double-occupied. Signals show red (next block occupied / no route), yellow (next block clear, the one after isn't), green (clear for two blocks). Trains brake to stop before red, slow for yellow. At junctions the train requests the route through the switch (e.g. via the passing loop); switches move and lock while reserved and release behind. Two trains on the single-track section are direction-locked: a train may only enter a route if it fully reserves it, otherwise it waits at the signal.
