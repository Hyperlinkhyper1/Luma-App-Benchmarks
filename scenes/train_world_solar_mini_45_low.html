How the signalling works

Blocks. The track is divided into blocks. A block may hold at most one train at a time — every car the train has puts it in that block. When any part of a train is in a block, the block is occupied and no other train may enter.

Three aspects. Every signal shows:

  • RED — the block ahead is occupied, or no route is set.
  • YELLOW — the block ahead is clear, but the signal after it is red.
  • GREEN — clear for at least two blocks ahead.

Routes. Before a signal in front of a switch can clear, the route through that switch is requested and reserved. The switch blade animates and locks while the route is held. The route releases behind the last train on it, and conflicting routes can never be reserved at the same time — that is why switches never foul each other.

Single track. The single-track section is one direction-locked route. Only one train may hold it, in one direction. A train wanting the other way must wait in the passing loop until it is released. The system re-plans itself every cycle, so it cannot deadlock — trains never stop permanently.

Interlocking. A route reservation covers every block, switch and signal it passes. No route may be reserved that overlaps a block already reserved by another route. Trains brake to a stand behind red signals, creep through yellow, and only proceed when the aspect and route allow.
